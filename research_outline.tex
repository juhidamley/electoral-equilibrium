\begin{abstract}
\stub{Skeleton --- fill after final runs.}
We present a three-stage framework that estimates a party's stable
\emph{equilibrium} voter coalition --- the race-bloc weighting that maximizes
win probability given baseline bloc loyalties --- and measures how a
hypothetical political shock shifts that coalition's effective loyalty and win
probability. A fine-tuned language model maps free-text shock descriptions to
per-bloc loyalty shifts; a quasi-convex optimizer recovers the equilibrium
coalition weights, computed once per party rather than once per shock; and a
compositional Monte Carlo returns a calibrated win-probability confidence
interval. Two methodological contributions anchor the paper: a \emph{neutrality
validation} protocol that separates structural (baseline) partisan bias from
model-driven bias, and an empirical \emph{coalition-stability} finding
(Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}) that, under demographically plausible coalition
bounds, the equilibrium coalition is invariant to single-shock effects ---
weights first move at a delta magnitude ($\sim$0.0633) that exceeds the model's
hard clip ceiling (0.0375) and so is unreachable at any legal shock intensity.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Writable from DECISIONS.md / DOMAIN.md / devlogs.
\textbf{Problem.} Political shocks (scandals, endorsements, economic news,
institutional events) re-sort the electorate: demographic blocs shift their
support unevenly. We formalize a party's most defensible strategic coalition as
an \emph{equilibrium}: the race-bloc weighting that maximizes win probability
given baseline bloc loyalties, subject to demographically plausible bounds,
computed once per party rather than once per shock. We then ask how a shock's
predicted per-bloc loyalty shifts move that fixed equilibrium coalition's
effective loyalty relative to an electoral-college--adjusted win threshold ---
not whether the coalition weighting itself should change in response, which it
empirically does not (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}).

\textbf{Data-scarcity gap.} There are only $\sim$20 U.S.\ presidential cycles
(1948--2024), so the shock $\rightarrow$ response mapping cannot be learned by
brute-force supervised regression on historical elections alone. We bridge this
with (i) survey-derived demographic panels, (ii) social-media supervision, and
(iii) synthetic shock--response data, and we push all small-sample uncertainty
into an explicit covariance term rather than hiding it.

\textbf{Research question.} \emph{Given a hypothetical shock, what is the optimal
race-bloc coalition weighting, and with what probability does the resulting
coalition win --- and is that estimate partisan-neutral?}

\textbf{Contributions.} In the order the paper substantiates them:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Magnitude miscalibration, found and corrected.} The model's
        predicted delta magnitudes were overstated $\sim$4$\times$ (mean
        cellwise $|$predicted/measured$|$ ratio 3.44) against real
        panel-measured within-person shifts; the decode table was rescaled to
        $\pm0.03$, anchored on the largest single-shock per-bloc shift ever
        measured in the panel (0.0286), reducing the ratio to 0.86
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
  \item \textbf{Panels measure scale but not per-bloc direction.} The VOTER
        panel is the only true within-person ground-truth layer, yet only
        19/270 (shock, bloc) cells pass the direction-trustworthiness filter
        --- 3 independent temporal windows, 53\% concentrated in two blocs ---
        a statistical-power failure, not a window-quality one
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
  \item \textbf{Miscalibration masquerading as sensitivity.} The pre-rescale
        overstatement pushed the optimizer and Monte Carlo past bounds they
        were never designed to operate near, producing apparent large
        shock-driven win-probability movement that was most likely a
        calibration artifact rather than genuine shock sensitivity ---
        corrected once the decode table was rescaled (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
  \item \textbf{Coalition stability, stated as a finding.} Under
        demographically plausible coalition bounds ($0.5$--$1.5\times$ each
        bloc's population share), the equilibrium coalition is invariant to
        single-shock effects (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}).
  \item \textbf{The instability threshold exceeds the effect size.}
        Coalition weights first move at a delta magnitude of $\sim$0.0633,
        while the model's hard clip ceiling is 0.0375 --- the margin by which
        no legally-scaled shock can ever destabilize the equilibrium
        coalition is itself a measured quantity, not an assumption
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}).
  \item \textbf{Nothing is solely attributable.} No ground-truth layer ---
        panel, CES, or exit poll --- has a single shock marked
        \texttt{solely\_attributable}: survey layers measure windows spanning
        multiple co-occurring events, while the model predicts one event at a
        time, a structural unit mismatch between validation data and
        prediction target (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}).
  \item \textbf{Social media's structural blind spots, disclosed rather than
        smoothed over.} A tiered inclusion discipline (bio-inferred posts feed
        mean \emph{and} covariance; group/platform proxies feed mean only;
        language-fallback posts are held out entirely) is paired with an
        explicit accounting of platform- and engagement-driven sample bias.
  \item \textbf{The system: a constrained-decoding LLM, a disciplined
        quasi-convex (DQCP) max-$P(\text{win})$ optimizer with
        demographic-plausibility bounds, and a Logistic-Normal ILR Monte
        Carlo} for calibrated win-probability confidence intervals ---
        a hybrid of language-model shock interpretation and portfolio-theory
        coalition optimization.
  \item \textbf{A neutrality-validation protocol} (zero-shock null test +
        matched-pair symmetry test) that isolates and removes a structural
        baseline bias and attributes the residual lean to base-model priors
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:neutrality}).
  \item \textbf{The SRP's negative results, reported rather than
        suppressed.} An ungrounded design assumption (the delta-bin range),
        a mischaracterized system property (shock-responsive coalition
        weights), and a validation gap (direction, pending elicitation) were
        each found, measured, and corrected or disclosed in turn --- reported
        here as findings in their own right, not edited out of the record.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Survey sources and panel construction}
The pipeline uses \textbf{two distinct demographic inputs}, which must not be
conflated:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Per-bloc loyalty $\mu$ --- a multi-source survey merge.}
        Within-bloc Democratic vote shares are estimated by merging several
        independent surveys: the National Election Pool (NEP) exit polls, the ANES
        Cumulative Data File (1948--2024), the General Social Survey (GSS), and the
        Cooperative Election Study (CES, 2006--2024). Each survey is first
        aggregated to a bloc-level share \emph{using its own sampling weights}
        (with Kish effective-$N$ standard errors), and the sources are then
        reconciled per cycle$\times$bloc cell by \textbf{inverse--standard-error
        weighting} (\texttt{resolve\_conflicts}: the more precise survey counts
        more). The Democracy Fund VOTER Study Group panel and Pew NPORS are wired
        into the loader but \emph{deferred} (wave-column parsing / party-proxy
        only), so they do not yet enter the merge.
  \item \textbf{Electorate composition $w^0$ --- NEP exit-poll shares.} The
        baseline \emph{size} of each race bloc, used to form the baseline coalition
        weights $w^0$ and the demographic-plausibility bounds in Stage~5, comes
        from \textbf{NEP exit-poll electorate shares} (White $0.63$, Latino
        $0.15$, African American $0.12$, Asian $0.05$, other $0.05$), \emph{not}
        from the survey merge. Loyalty $\mu$ answers ``how Democratic is each
        bloc''; $w^0$ answers ``how large is each bloc'' --- two separate
        quantities.
\end{itemize}
The panel is stored as three parallel, non-nested strata (race / religion /
gender), each marginalizing to $\sim$100\% of the electorate.

\subsection{Imputation and data-quality rules}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{1992 Perot three-party correction:} raw two-party shares are
        corrected using \emph{bloc-specific} 1992 exit-poll Perot support
        (White 21\%, African American 7\%, Latino 14\%), not a flat national
        adjustment.
  \item \textbf{ANES 2020 weights:} ANES is one of several merged loyalty sources;
        its 2020 sampling-weight column is entirely null, so that cycle falls back
        to equal-weighting, inflating some estimates (e.g.\ White Dem share
        $\sim$3.6\,pp above NEP). It is down-weighted / excluded from the affected
        race and gender merges --- a real data-quality limitation, not silently
        corrected.
  % CONFLICT: earlier decisions treated some pre-1990 imputed cells (e.g.
  % other_race 1948 carry-forward from 1952) as usable; the 2026-06-29
  % covariance window later EXCLUDED them. Used newer (exclusion). See sec:cov.
  \item \textbf{Thin-bloc imputation, later superseded:} early panel work
        carried forward sparse pre-1990 cells (e.g.\ \texttt{other\_race}
        1948--1956 carry-forward; an empty 1968 ANES \texttt{asian} cell). These
        are \emph{excluded} by the current 1990+ covariance window
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:cov}); do not present the earlier carry-forward as
        current.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Social-media supervision}
Social posts provide RoBERTa sentiment-elasticity signal, tiered by how
demographics are inferred, with strict rules on what may enter the covariance
$\Sigma_\Delta$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{user\_bio} (Tier 1; Bluesky, X/Apify): SetFit bio classifier;
        included in both the mean $\mu$ \emph{and} $\Sigma_\Delta$.
  \item \texttt{subreddit\_proxy} / \texttt{platform\_proxy} (Reddit;
        TruthSocial, TikTok, 3DLNews): group/platform-level proxies; included in
        $\mu$ only, \textbf{excluded from} $\Sigma_\Delta$ (ecological-fallacy
        guard).
  \item \texttt{language\_prior} (fallback): \textbf{excluded from both} $\mu$
        and $\Sigma_\Delta$ (held-out only); mixing it would bifurcate the
        mean/covariance data sources and violate optimizer assumptions.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Sample-bias caveats.} Social platforms over-represent the
politically engaged and skew by platform (TruthSocial conservative; TikTok
young/diverse); surveys under-represent low-response strata. These are carried
as limitations, not silently corrected.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Overview}
Seven-stage pipeline: (1) voter panel $\rightarrow$ (2) baseline portfolio
$\rightarrow$ (3a--d) sentiment / finetune / market $\rightarrow$ (4) LLM shock
response $\rightarrow$ (5) DQCP optimization $\rightarrow$ (6) Monte Carlo.
Deterministic under a single global seed (42).

\subsection{Stage 4 --- fine-tuned shock model}\label{sec:stage4}
Base model \texttt{mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.3}, QLoRA rank $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$, target modules
\{\texttt{q\_proj},\texttt{k\_proj},\texttt{v\_proj},\texttt{o\_proj}\},
4-bit NF4, lr $2\times10^{-4}$, 3 epochs, batch 4. The \textbf{primary} output is
JSON over 15 blocs, each assigned one of \textbf{9 ordinal delta bins}
(\texttt{strong\_neg} $\dots$ \texttt{strong\_pos}) tiling the clipped range
$[-0.0375,+0.0375]$ (midpoints run $\pm0.03$ at the extremes).
% CONFLICT: the decode table originally tiled [-0.15,+0.15] (midpoints
% +-0.12) -- an ungrounded design assumption fixed before any ground-truth
% extraction existed. Rescaled (Step 2.1, DECISIONS.md) to +-0.03, anchored
% on the largest single-shock per-bloc delta ever measured in the VOTER
% panel (0.0286); the +-0.0375 clip preserves the same ratio between the
% clip and the bin ceiling as the old range did. See Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}
% for the calibration-ratio validation (3.44x -> 0.86x). Used newer
% (rescaled) throughout this paper.
A scalar
\texttt{delta\_eff} is a \emph{secondary, derived} summary --- the
$\lambda$-weighted blend of the per-bloc deltas --- not an independent
prediction. \textbf{Constrained decoding} via \texttt{outlines} (Pydantic
FSM) guarantees schema-valid tokens; greedy+regex is the fallback.

\subsection{Stage 5 --- DQCP max-$P(\text{win})$ optimizer}
Decision variables are the 5 race-bloc weights $w$ (simplex). Objective:
\[
\max_{w}\ \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)-V_{eq}}
{\lambda_1\sqrt{w^{\top}\Sigma_\Delta w}}\right),
\qquad
\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)=\lambda_1\!\sum_i w_i\tilde\mu_i^{\text{race}}
+\lambda_2\!\sum_R v_R\tilde\mu_R^{\text{rel}}
+\lambda_3\!\sum_G g_G\tilde\mu_G^{\text{gen}} .
\]
$\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF (monotone), so this is a Sharpe-ratio form.
\textbf{Quasiconvexity:} numerator affine, denominator convex-positive
$\Rightarrow$ ratio quasi-convex (Diamond \& Boyd 2019); solved as an SOCP via
Charnes--Cooper (CLARABEL). \textbf{Bounds:} each bloc constrained to
$[0.5\,w^0_i,\,1.5\,w^0_i]$ around its baseline NEP share (demographic
plausibility). \textbf{Win condition:} $\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)\ge V_{eq}$.
Because the gap between blocs' baseline loyalties dominates any shock-scale
perturbation to $\mu_{\text{eff}}$, $w^{*}$ is empirically stable across
shocks within these bounds (Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}): the optimizer is run
once per party, and the shock is evaluated against that fixed $w^{*}$, not
the other way around.

\subsection{Stage 6 --- Logistic-Normal ILR Monte Carlo}
Not Dirichlet (forbids the positive off-diagonal covariance of wave elections)
and not the delta method. Steps: (1) ILR forward $z^{*}=V^{\top}\log w^{*}$ with
Helmert contrast $V$; (2) propagate $\Sigma_{\text{ILR}}=J\Sigma_\Delta J^{\top}$,
$J=V^{\top}\mathrm{diag}(1/w^{*})$; (3) draw $y^{(n)}\sim\mathcal N(z^{*},
\Sigma_{\text{ILR}})$, $N{=}10{,}000$; (4) back-transform
$w^{(n)}=\mathrm{softmax}(Vy^{(n)})$; (5) win fraction and 5th/95th-percentile
CI. PSD repair floors eigenvalues at $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Covariance $\Sigma_\Delta$}\label{sec:cov}
Race-only $5\times5$, estimated by \textbf{Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage}.
% CONFLICT: 20-cycle 1948-2024 (docs/decisions.md:119-145; week2 devlogs) vs
% 9-cycle 1992-2024, 8 first-differences (DECISIONS.md:1337, 2026-06-29).
% Used newer (9-cycle 1992+).
Estimated over \textbf{9 cycles (1992--2024, 8 first-differences)}, restricted
from the full 1948--2024 panel: pre-1990 cycles are excluded because thin blocs
(\texttt{asian}, \texttt{latino}, \texttt{other\_race}) had sparse/imputed
sampling that inflated their variance $>2\times$ (e.g.\ \texttt{asian}
$0.27\!\rightarrow\!0.12$). Shrinkage mitigates but does not eliminate the

\subsection{Zero-shock null test}
Feeding all-zero deltas through the full equilibrium + simulation for both
parties isolates \emph{structural} baseline bias (independent of any LLM output).
The pre-fix null was strongly asymmetric:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Party & $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ & margin & win prob. \\
\midrule
Democrat   & 0.5575 & $+0.0509$ & 1.00 \\
Republican & 0.5000 & $+0.0066$ & 0.26 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Root cause and fix}\label{sec:rootcause}
The bias was \emph{not} in the ML model but in an \textbf{equal-within-stratum
weighting placeholder} that over-weighted small, strongly-Democratic religion/
gender blocs (\texttt{jewish},\texttt{muslim},\texttt{other\_gender}) by
$7$--$33\times$. Replacing it with real electorate marginals moved the Democratic
religion mean $0.543\!\rightarrow\!0.499$ and gender mean
$0.567\!\rightarrow\!0.505$, cutting structural asymmetry
$+0.052\!\rightarrow\!+0.001$ (\textbf{$\sim$98.2\% reduction}). The small
residual slightly favors Republicans --- the intended EC-adjusted-target effect.

\subsection{Residual win-probability asymmetry}
A residual win-probability asymmetry is \emph{retained by design} as a
coalition-\emph{concentration} property: the two parties' baseline bloc
loyalties are ordered differently (e.g.\ \texttt{white} is the
highest-loyalty bloc under Republican framing and the lowest under
Democratic framing), so the equilibrium coalition's demographic-plausibility
bounds bind on different blocs per party, not a structural mean bias. This
is a \emph{cross-party} property of the equilibrium coalition, distinct from
--- and not in tension with --- the \emph{cross-shock} stability finding of
Sec.~\ref{sec:stability}: the equilibrium coalition differs by which party
is being modeled, but does not move in response to any single shock once a
party is fixed.

\subsection{Matched-pair symmetry test and elimination}
Matched party-flipped shock pairs reveal a residual behavioral lean of

\section{Coalition Stability}\label{sec:stability}
% Canonical framing lifted from docs/design/optimizer_framing.md (Step 3.1,
% Option C decision) -- see DECISIONS.md "Step 3.1" for the full record.
The optimizer computes an \emph{equilibrium coalition}: the race-bloc
weighting $w^{*}$ that maximizes a party's win probability given that
party's baseline bloc loyalties and $\Sigma_\Delta$, subject to the
demographic-plausibility bounds of Sec.~\ref{sec:cov}. This is computed
\textbf{once per party, not once per shock} --- it is a property of the
party's standing coalition, not of any single hypothetical event. A shock is
not asked to move $w^{*}$; it is evaluated \emph{against} the fixed
equilibrium coalition, through $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ (which moves linearly with
the shock) and win probability (which moves nonlinearly, and only when the
baseline margin over $V_{eq}$ is tight enough for the effect to be visible
against Monte Carlo noise).

\textbf{Finding, stated positively:} under demographically plausible
coalition constraints, the optimal coalition is stable against single-shock
effects. This is a substantive result about electoral coalitions, not a
limitation to disclose apologetically --- a campaign's demographically
bounded, risk-adjusted strategic emphasis does not rationally re-litigate
itself after every single news event. A shock-responsive optimizer would
have been the less defensible and less interesting result here.

\textbf{Empirical basis.} Bisection directly on the optimizer's solve found
that coalition weights first move at a delta magnitude of
\textbf{$\sim$0.0633}, while the hard clip ceiling on any shock's per-bloc
delta is \textbf{0.0375} --- a full \textbf{1.7$\times$ below} the movement
threshold, unreachable at any legal shock intensity. This was confirmed
three independent ways, none of which moved the equilibrium weights:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Adversarial synthetic construction} --- every race bloc
        pinned to its most extreme decode bin simultaneously, with intensity
        multipliers swept up to $10\times$ (far past the API's legal
        maximum of $3\times$).
  \item \textbf{Real model inference at the API's maximum legal intensity}
        ($3\times$) on real shocks, real party framing.
  \item \textbf{Both party framings} --- Democrat and Republican baselines
        land at different corners of the demographic-plausibility bound
        (consistent with each party's different bloc-loyalty ordering, see
        Sec.~\ref{sec:neutrality}), but within either framing no tested
        shock moved weights off that party's own corner.
\end{enumerate}
Meanwhile $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ and win probability \emph{do} respond: under a
tighter-margin baseline (Republican framing), mean win-probability movement
across tested shocks was $0.0503$, with real (non-degenerate)
confidence-interval widths of roughly $0.012$--$0.015$ --- not a saturated
point estimate. The shock-conditional signal is real; it lives downstream of
the coalition weights, not in them.

\textbf{The threshold, made interpretable.} Stated as a bare number, 0.0633
invites the question ``large compared to what?'' Compared to what shocks
actually do: it is \textbf{$\sim$22$\times$} the panel's median measured
single-shock, single-bloc effect ($0.00287$, $n{=}234$ non-suppressed cells),
\textbf{$\sim$14$\times$} the mean ($0.00461$), \textbf{$\sim$5.6$\times$}
the 90th percentile ($0.01123$), and \textbf{$\sim$2.2$\times$} the single
largest per-bloc shock effect ever measured in the panel across every shock
in the ground truth ($0.02865$). Even the model's own maximum possible
output --- the hard clip ceiling, $0.0375$, reachable only at the API's
highest legal intensity --- is itself just $1.3\times$ the largest historical
shock, and still $1.7\times$ below the movement threshold. This converts
``the weights never move'' from an assertion into a falsifiable comparison:
moving the equilibrium coalition would require a single-shock, single-bloc
effect more than double the largest one this project's own ground truth has
ever recorded.

\textbf{The substantive claim.} Read as a claim about American electoral
coalitions, not about this model's arithmetic: \emph{coalition strategy is
set by the structural, slow-moving gap between how loyal different
demographic blocs already are to a party, not by the fast-moving news cycle
of individual shocks.} The quantity that would have to change for a party's
optimal strategic emphasis to shift is not ``did something happen this
week'' but ``has the underlying loyalty gap between, say, the highest- and
lowest-loyalty race blocs (currently $\sim$0.94 vs.\ $\sim$0.45, a
$0.49$-point gap under Democratic framing) itself narrowed or widened'' ---
and that is a claim about years-to-decades-scale partisan change, not about
any single event. This is a claim someone could disagree with: it implies
that no single scandal, endorsement, or crisis --- however large its
immediate loyalty effect --- is by itself enough to make it strategically
rational for a party to reallocate emphasis away from its most loyal blocs
and toward its least loyal ones. Disagreeing with it would require showing
either that real shocks can produce loyalty effects far larger than anything
in this project's ground truth, or that the demographic-plausibility bounds
constraining the model are themselves wrong.

\textbf{What is substantive versus what is artifactual.} This claim
decomposes into two parts with different epistemic status, and the paper is
explicit about which is which rather than letting the stronger one borrow
credibility from the weaker one. The \emph{substantive} part --- that the
baseline loyalty spread between blocs dwarfs any single-shock effect --- is
a property of the measured world, independent of the $0.5$--$1.5\times$
bounds: the $0.49$-point loyalty gap and the panel-measured shock effects
are separately-estimated empirical quantities, and their ratio is large by
any reasonable standard ($\sim$171$\times$ the median shock effect,
$\sim$17$\times$ even the single largest shock ever measured). No plausible
choice of demographic-plausibility bound changes the fact that a shock is
two orders of magnitude smaller than the loyalty gap it would need to
overcome. The \emph{artifactual} part is the specific $0.0633$ threshold
value and its exact $1.7\times$ margin over the $0.0375$ clip ceiling: both
are a direct function of the $0.5\times/1.5\times$ bounds in
\texttt{cvx.py}, documented in-source as ``PROVISIONAL $\dots$ pending
advisor review, not empirically calibrated.'' Widening those bounds would
mechanically lower the movement threshold --- a looser bound gives the
optimizer more room to reallocate before hitting a corner. The
\emph{direction} of the finding (a large stability margin) would very
likely survive any bound choice that remains demographically plausible in
any ordinary sense, precisely because the underlying loyalty-gap-to-shock
ratio is so large; but the \emph{exact} threshold, and any claim of the form
``weights are invariant, full stop,'' is contingent on this specific,
unreviewed parameter and should not be stated as parameter-free.

\textbf{Alternative considered and rejected.} Widening the
demographic-plausibility bounds until weights move was rejected for the same
reason: this would mean choosing a bound because it makes the output move,
not because it reflects a defensible constraint --- the identical reasoning
that produced the original ungrounded $\pm0.15$ delta-bin range
(Sec.~\ref{sec:validation}). These bounds remain marked provisional pending
advisor review; this finding does not resolve that review, it declines to
change the bounds as a side effect of a framing correction.

\textbf{Situating the claim in the literature.} This finding aligns most
directly with the social-identity account of partisanship (Green, Palmquist
\& Schickler 2002), which holds that partisan identities are formed early
in adulthood and change, like religious identities, only slowly rather than
with each news cycle, and with the original Michigan-school claim that party
identification is a stable predisposition through which voters filter
short-term political information (Campbell, Converse, Miller \& Stokes
1960). It also echoes V.O.\ Key's account of coalition change as
concentrated in rare ``critical'' realigning elections rather than
distributed continuously across ordinary events (Key 1955) --- though
Mayhew's later critique (2002) disputes that clean, periodized realignments
are empirically real at all, so this paper leans only on Key's descriptive
claim (coalitions are usually stable, occasionally not) without endorsing
the stronger realignment-cycle theory built on top of it.

Two bodies of work complicate this picture and are reported rather than
omitted. First, MacKuen, Erikson \& Stimson's ``Macropartisanship'' (1989)
found that \emph{aggregate} party identification, measured quarterly rather
than biennially, shows substantial and systematic short-term movement
tracking presidential approval and the economy --- a real finding of
short-term responsiveness in a quantity this paper otherwise treats as
slow-moving. Second, Achen \& Bartels' ``blind retrospection'' findings
(2016, Ch.~5) are a direct counter-example at the level of individual
events: Wilson lost up to ten points of support in coastal New Jersey
counties after a spate of shark attacks in 1916, and droughts have been
found to cost incumbents $0.7$--$1.5$ percentage points --- the shark-attack
figure alone exceeds even the single largest effect in this paper's own
ground truth ($0.029$). A third, smaller example --- Healy, Malhotra \&
Mo's (2010) finding that a college-football win shifts incumbent vote share
by roughly $1.6$ percentage points, close to this paper's own 90th
percentile ($0.011$) --- was later reassessed by Fowler \& Montagnes (2015),
who found the effect does not replicate and is likely a false positive.

These findings do not straightforwardly overturn the claim here, for two
reasons, stated rather than assumed. First, most are about aggregate vote
share or election-day behavior, not the within-person, single-shock
bloc-loyalty quantity this paper's ground truth measures --- Achen and
Bartels' droughts finding ($0.7$--$1.5$pp) is, in fact, the same order of
magnitude as this paper's own 90th percentile ($1.1$pp), not orders of
magnitude larger; only the single shark-attack anecdote is a genuine
outlier at this project's scale. Second, this paper's claim is about
whether a shock changes which coalition \emph{strategy} is optimal, not
about whether a shock moves vote share at all --- MacKuen et al.'s quarterly
movement in macropartisanship, real as it is, has not been shown to be
large enough at the per-bloc level to overturn which blocs a party should
most heavily emphasize. The honest summary: this paper's finding is
consistent with the bulk of the partisan-stability literature and with the
typical magnitude of the retrospective-voting literature, but the
shark-attack result specifically shows that ``no single event moves a
coalition this much'' is an empirical regularity in this project's data and
time period, not a law of politics --- a sufficiently unusual, geographically
concentrated event could in principle exceed it.

\textbf{Connection to the magnitude finding.} The coalition-stability
finding and the magnitude-calibration finding (Sec.~\ref{sec:validation})
are two views of one underlying fact, not two separate results. The
magnitude finding says single-shock effects on bloc loyalty are small in
absolute terms --- a median of $0.003$, a 90th percentile of $0.011$, a
maximum ever measured of $0.029$ --- once the model's overstated
$3.44\times$-inflated predictions are corrected to match panel-measured
reality. The stability finding says the optimal coalition only responds to
bloc-loyalty effects an order of magnitude larger than that: $0.0633$,
roughly $22\times$ the median and $2.2\times$ the largest real effect on
record. Together they describe a single phenomenon from two angles: single
political events move electoral coalitions by a small, now-correctly-measured
amount, and that amount is consistently, measurably too small to change
which strategic coalition is optimal. The pre-rescale model got both halves
of this story wrong in the same direction --- it overstated shock effects
roughly $4\times$, which pushed the optimizer close enough to its
bound-driven movement threshold to produce what looked like genuine
coalition responsiveness. Correcting the scale did not just fix a
calibration number; it resolved an apparent behavior (a shock-responsive
optimizer) that the corrected numbers show was never really there.

\section{Ground-Truth Validation: Two Problems}\label{sec:validation}
Ground-truth validation against three survey layers (VOTER panel, CES,
exit polls; ranked by fidelity, never pooled) separates into two problems
with very different status.

\subsection{Magnitude/calibration --- solved, panel-anchored}
The pre-rescale decode table ($\pm0.15$, Sec.~\ref{sec:stage4})
was an ungrounded design assumption, fixed before any ground-truth
extraction existed. Scored against the VOTER panel's magnitude-usable cells,
the mean cellwise $|$predicted/measured$|$ ratio was \textbf{3.44$\times$}
--- the model's predicted magnitudes were, on average, more than three times
larger than what the panel actually measured. The decode table was rescaled
to $\pm0.03$ (clip $\pm0.0375$), anchored on the largest single-shock
per-bloc delta ever measured in the panel (\textbf{0.0286}), not on a
percentile --- a percentile anchor would have made the largest real shocks
in the ground truth unrepresentable. Re-scored, the ratio falls to
\textbf{0.86$\times$}, close to unbiased.

$\Sigma_\Delta$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:cov}) was \emph{already} estimated directly
from real historical vote-share first-differences, in real vote-share units
--- it has no dependency on the decode table at all. The rescale therefore
did not introduce a new mismatch between the prediction scale and the
uncertainty model; it resolved a \emph{pre-existing} one, in which an
overstated prediction scale and an already-correctly-scaled uncertainty
model were combined without either being checked against the other.

\subsection{Direction --- not solved; depends on expert elicitation}
Per-bloc \emph{direction} validation is a substantially harder problem.
Only \textbf{19 of 270} (shock, bloc) panel cells pass the
direction-trustworthiness filter (sign-stable, statistically significant,
not suppressed, single-shock attributable), spanning just \textbf{3}
independent temporal windows and \textbf{53\%} concentrated in two blocs
(\texttt{white}, \texttt{catholic}). This is a \textbf{statistical-power
failure, not a window-quality failure}: even the tightest, cleanest
bracket only clears the direction bar for one bloc. CES and exit-poll
layers are magnitude-only by construction and never direction-eligible.
\textbf{No ground-truth layer has any shock marked
\texttt{solely\_attributable}}: survey layers measure windows spanning
multiple co-occurring events, while the model predicts one event at a
time --- a structural unit mismatch between the validation data and the
prediction target, not an artifact of any one extraction choice.

Because the panel cannot support per-bloc direction validation at scale, a
blinded expert-elicitation protocol was designed as the primary
direction-validation route: 12 shocks (drawn from the held-out set),
independently-written neutral descriptions, magnitude buckets anchored to
the rescaled $\pm0.03$ range, pre-registered agreement thresholds (Fleiss'
$\kappa\ge0.40$ gates the analysis before any model comparison is
computed). \textbf{The protocol is designed and its scoring code
self-tested; it has not yet been run.}

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Additive independence / ecological fallacy} (Robinson 1950): the
        three strata are combined additively with no cross-tabulation; $\mu$ and
        $V_{eq}$ coincide only under this approximation.
  \item \textbf{Model-internal win threshold:} $V_{eq}$ is an EC-adjusted
        effective-loyalty threshold, not a literal popular-vote count.
  \item \textbf{Residual base-model lean ($+0.028$):} survives corpus rebalancing;
        disclosed rather than tuned away.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic-label provenance:} labels are LLM-generated
        (DeepSeek+Gemini); external-API use is a documented deviation from the
        reproducibility rule for this stage.
  \item \textbf{Race-only rebalancing:} only 5 race blocs are optimized; religion/
        gender are fixed weights, and the Monte Carlo treats them as neutral
        ($\mu{=}0.50$), understating uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Small-sample covariance:} 8 first-differences even after
        shrinkage; thin blocs remain data-limited.
\end{itemize}
