\begin{abstract}
\stub{Skeleton --- fill after final runs.}
We present a three-stage framework that estimates how a party's voter coalition
must rebalance after a hypothetical political shock. A fine-tuned language model
maps free-text shock descriptions to per-bloc loyalty shifts; a quasi-convex
optimizer recovers the coalition weights that maximize win probability; and a
compositional Monte Carlo returns a calibrated confidence interval. Our central
methodological contribution is a \emph{neutrality validation} protocol that
separates structural (baseline) partisan bias from model-driven bias.

$\sim$98\%, residual base-model lean $+0.028$, final MAE.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% Writable from DECISIONS.md / DOMAIN.md / devlogs.
\textbf{Problem.} Political shocks (scandals, endorsements, economic news,
institutional events) re-sort the electorate: demographic blocs shift their
support unevenly, so a campaign's \emph{optimal} coalition emphasis changes
after a shock. We formalize this as recovering post-shock coalition weights that
keep a party's effective loyalty above an electoral-college--adjusted win
threshold.

\textbf{Data-scarcity gap.} There are only $\sim$20 U.S.\ presidential cycles
(1948--2024), so the shock $\rightarrow$ response mapping cannot be learned by
brute-force supervised regression on historical elections alone. We bridge this
with (i) survey-derived demographic panels, (ii) social-media supervision, and
(iii) synthetic shock--response data, and we push all small-sample uncertainty
into an explicit covariance term rather than hiding it.

\textbf{Research question.} \emph{Given a hypothetical shock, what is the optimal
race-bloc coalition weighting, and with what probability does the resulting
coalition win --- and is that estimate partisan-neutral?}

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A constrained-decoding LLM stage that emits ordinal per-bloc loyalty
        shifts over 15 demographic blocs (5 race, 7 religion, 3 gender).
  \item A disciplined quasi-convex (DQCP) max-$P(\text{win})$ optimizer with
        demographic-plausibility bounds, plus a Logistic-Normal ILR Monte Carlo
        for calibrated win-probability confidence intervals.
  \item A neutrality-validation protocol (zero-shock null test + matched-pair
        symmetry test) that isolates and removes a structural baseline bias and
        attributes the residual lean to base-model priors.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data}
% Writable from Data Sources / Ingest / Synthetic sections.
\subsection{Survey sources and panel construction}
The pipeline uses \textbf{two distinct demographic inputs}, which must not be
conflated:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Per-bloc loyalty $\mu$ --- a multi-source survey merge.}
        Within-bloc Democratic vote shares are estimated by merging several
        independent surveys: the National Election Pool (NEP) exit polls, the ANES
        Cumulative Data File (1948--2024), the General Social Survey (GSS), and the
        Cooperative Election Study (CES, 2006--2024). Each survey is first
        aggregated to a bloc-level share \emph{using its own sampling weights}
        (with Kish effective-$N$ standard errors), and the sources are then
        reconciled per cycle$\times$bloc cell by \textbf{inverse--standard-error
        weighting} (\texttt{resolve\_conflicts}: the more precise survey counts
        more). The Democracy Fund VOTER Study Group panel and Pew NPORS are wired
        into the loader but \emph{deferred} (wave-column parsing / party-proxy
        only), so they do not yet enter the merge.
  \item \textbf{Electorate composition $w^0$ --- NEP exit-poll shares.} The
        baseline \emph{size} of each race bloc, used to form the baseline coalition
        weights $w^0$ and the demographic-plausibility bounds in Stage~5, comes
        from \textbf{NEP exit-poll electorate shares} (White $0.63$, Latino
        $0.15$, African American $0.12$, Asian $0.05$, other $0.05$), \emph{not}
        from the survey merge. Loyalty $\mu$ answers ``how Democratic is each
        bloc''; $w^0$ answers ``how large is each bloc'' --- two separate
        quantities.
\end{itemize}
The panel is stored as three parallel, non-nested strata (race / religion /
gender), each marginalizing to $\sim$100\% of the electorate.

\subsection{Imputation and data-quality rules}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{1992 Perot three-party correction:} raw two-party shares are
        corrected using \emph{bloc-specific} 1992 exit-poll Perot support
        (White 21\%, African American 7\%, Latino 14\%), not a flat national
        adjustment.
  \item \textbf{ANES 2020 weights:} ANES is one of several merged loyalty sources;
        its 2020 sampling-weight column is entirely null, so that cycle falls back
        to equal-weighting, inflating some estimates (e.g.\ White Dem share
        $\sim$3.6\,pp above NEP). It is down-weighted / excluded from the affected
        race and gender merges --- a real data-quality limitation, not silently
        corrected.
  % CONFLICT: earlier decisions treated some pre-1990 imputed cells (e.g.
  % other_race 1948 carry-forward from 1952) as usable; the 2026-06-29
  % covariance window later EXCLUDED them. Used newer (exclusion). See sec:cov.
  \item \textbf{Thin-bloc imputation, later superseded:} early panel work
        carried forward sparse pre-1990 cells (e.g.\ \texttt{other\_race}
        1948--1956 carry-forward; an empty 1968 ANES \texttt{asian} cell). These
        are \emph{excluded} by the current 1990+ covariance window
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:cov}); do not present the earlier carry-forward as
        current.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Social-media supervision}
Social posts provide RoBERTa sentiment-elasticity signal, tiered by how
demographics are inferred, with strict rules on what may enter the covariance
$\Sigma_\Delta$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{user\_bio} (Tier 1; Bluesky, X/Apify): SetFit bio classifier;
        included in both the mean $\mu$ \emph{and} $\Sigma_\Delta$.
  \item \texttt{subreddit\_proxy} / \texttt{platform\_proxy} (Reddit;
        TruthSocial, TikTok, 3DLNews): group/platform-level proxies; included in
        $\mu$ only, \textbf{excluded from} $\Sigma_\Delta$ (ecological-fallacy
        guard).
  \item \texttt{language\_prior} (fallback): \textbf{excluded from both} $\mu$
        and $\Sigma_\Delta$ (held-out only); mixing it would bifurcate the
        mean/covariance data sources and violate optimizer assumptions.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Sample-bias caveats.} Social platforms over-represent the
politically engaged and skew by platform (TruthSocial conservative; TikTok
young/diverse); surveys under-represent low-response strata. These are carried
as limitations, not silently corrected.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{Overview}
Seven-stage pipeline: (1) voter panel $\rightarrow$ (2) baseline portfolio
$\rightarrow$ (3a--d) sentiment / finetune / market $\rightarrow$ (4) LLM shock
response $\rightarrow$ (5) DQCP optimization $\rightarrow$ (6) Monte Carlo.
Deterministic under a single global seed (42).

\subsection{Stage 4 --- fine-tuned shock model}
Base model \texttt{mistralai/Mistral-7B-v0.3}, QLoRA rank $r{=}16$,
$\alpha{=}32$, dropout $0.05$, target modules
\{\texttt{q\_proj},\texttt{k\_proj},\texttt{v\_proj},\texttt{o\_proj}\},
4-bit NF4, lr $2\times10^{-4}$, 3 epochs, batch 4. The \textbf{primary} output is
JSON over 15 blocs, each assigned one of \textbf{9 ordinal delta bins}
(\texttt{strong\_neg} $\dots$ \texttt{strong\_pos}) tiling the clipped range
$[-0.15,+0.15]$. A scalar
\texttt{delta\_eff} is a \emph{secondary, derived} summary --- the
$\lambda$-weighted blend of the per-bloc deltas --- not an independent
prediction. \textbf{Constrained decoding} via \texttt{outlines} (Pydantic
FSM) guarantees schema-valid tokens; greedy+regex is the fallback.

\subsection{Stage 5 --- DQCP max-$P(\text{win})$ optimizer}
Decision variables are the 5 race-bloc weights $w$ (simplex). Objective:
\[
\max_{w}\ \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)-V_{eq}}
{\lambda_1\sqrt{w^{\top}\Sigma_\Delta w}}\right),
\qquad
\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)=\lambda_1\!\sum_i w_i\tilde\mu_i^{\text{race}}
+\lambda_2\!\sum_R v_R\tilde\mu_R^{\text{rel}}
+\lambda_3\!\sum_G g_G\tilde\mu_G^{\text{gen}} .
\]
$\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF (monotone), so this is a Sharpe-ratio form.
\textbf{Quasiconvexity:} numerator affine, denominator convex-positive
$\Rightarrow$ ratio quasi-convex (Diamond \& Boyd 2019); solved as an SOCP via
Charnes--Cooper (CLARABEL). \textbf{Bounds:} each bloc constrained to
$[0.5\,w^0_i,\,1.5\,w^0_i]$ around its baseline NEP share (demographic
plausibility). \textbf{Win condition:} $\mu_{\text{eff}}(w)\ge V_{eq}$.

% was replaced by real electorate marginals in Week 8 (see sec:rootcause).

\subsection{Layer weights and win threshold}
Layer weights $\lambda_{1,2,3}$: additive prior $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$; IPF-raked
posterior $(0.114,0.224,0.662)$ --- the paper compares additive vs raked. Win
threshold $V_{eq}$: EC-adjusted logistic regression gives $0.5066$ (Dem) /
$0.4934$ (Rep), with sensitivity bounds $[0.501,0.515]$ (Dem) and
$[0.485,0.499]$ (Rep).
% FIX 4: the two bound pairs are NOT in conflict --- 0.501/0.515 are the Democrat
% low/high and 0.485/0.499 the Republican low/high (configs/party_config.json).
% FIX 3: both raked lambda (raking.py: all cycles with full stratum coverage) and
% V_eq (party_config.json _methodology: fit on 20 cycles 1948-2024) use the FULL
% panel, verified against raking.py:199 and configs/party_config.json.
Both the raked $\lambda$ and $V_{eq}$ are calibrated on the \textbf{full
1948--2024 panel (20 cycles)} --- deliberately a wider window than the
covariance $\Sigma_\Delta$, which restricts to 1990+ (Sec.~\ref{sec:cov}): the
layer weights and win threshold want maximal historical leverage, whereas
$\Sigma_\Delta$ excludes pre-1990 cycles to avoid thin-bloc sampling artifacts.

\subsection{Stage 6 --- Logistic-Normal ILR Monte Carlo}
Not Dirichlet (forbids the positive off-diagonal covariance of wave elections)
and not the delta method. Steps: (1) ILR forward $z^{*}=V^{\top}\log w^{*}$ with
Helmert contrast $V$; (2) propagate $\Sigma_{\text{ILR}}=J\Sigma_\Delta J^{\top}$,
$J=V^{\top}\mathrm{diag}(1/w^{*})$; (3) draw $y^{(n)}\sim\mathcal N(z^{*},
\Sigma_{\text{ILR}})$, $N{=}10{,}000$; (4) back-transform
$w^{(n)}=\mathrm{softmax}(Vy^{(n)})$; (5) win fraction and 5th/95th-percentile
CI. PSD repair floors eigenvalues at $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Covariance $\Sigma_\Delta$}\label{sec:cov}
Race-only $5\times5$, estimated by \textbf{Ledoit--Wolf shrinkage}.
% CONFLICT: 20-cycle 1948-2024 (docs/decisions.md:119-145; week2 devlogs) vs
% 9-cycle 1992-2024, 8 first-differences (DECISIONS.md:1337, 2026-06-29).
% Used newer (9-cycle 1992+).
Estimated over \textbf{9 cycles (1992--2024, 8 first-differences)}, restricted
from the full 1948--2024 panel: pre-1990 cycles are excluded because thin blocs
(\texttt{asian}, \texttt{latino}, \texttt{other\_race}) had sparse/imputed
sampling that inflated their variance $>2\times$ (e.g.\ \texttt{asian}
$0.27\!\rightarrow\!0.12$). Shrinkage mitigates but does not eliminate the
small-sample limitation for the thin blocs.

\section{Neutrality Validation}\label{sec:neutrality}
% Strongest section --- writable from Week 8 devlog + DECISIONS.md (2026-06-29).
\subsection{Zero-shock null test}
Feeding all-zero deltas through the full equilibrium + simulation for both
parties isolates \emph{structural} baseline bias (independent of any LLM output).
The pre-fix null was strongly asymmetric:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Party & $\mu_{\text{eff}}$ & margin & win prob. \\
\midrule
Democrat   & 0.5575 & $+0.0509$ & 1.00 \\
Republican & 0.5000 & $+0.0066$ & 0.26 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Root cause and fix}\label{sec:rootcause}
The bias was \emph{not} in the ML model but in an \textbf{equal-within-stratum
weighting placeholder} that over-weighted small, strongly-Democratic religion/
gender blocs (\texttt{jewish},\texttt{muslim},\texttt{other\_gender}) by
$7$--$33\times$. Replacing it with real electorate marginals moved the Democratic
religion mean $0.543\!\rightarrow\!0.499$ and gender mean
$0.567\!\rightarrow\!0.505$, cutting structural asymmetry
$+0.052\!\rightarrow\!+0.001$ (\textbf{$\sim$98.2\% reduction}). The small
residual slightly favors Republicans --- the intended EC-adjusted-target effect.

\subsection{Residual win-probability asymmetry}
A residual win-probability asymmetry is \emph{retained by design} as a
coalition-\emph{concentration} property (the optimizer concentrates emphasis
differently per party), not a structural mean bias.

\subsection{Matched-pair symmetry test and elimination}
Matched party-flipped shock pairs reveal a residual behavioral lean of
\textbf{$+0.028$} (Democrat-favoring). Traced by elimination:
(i) baseline aggregation --- eliminated (Sec.~\ref{sec:rootcause});
(ii) covariance artifacts --- eliminated (Monte Carlo now consumes the real
$\Sigma_\Delta$);
(iii) synthetic-corpus label imbalance --- rebalanced to $-0.0007$ and the
adapter retrained, yet the lean \textbf{persisted at $+0.028$}.
\textbf{Conclusion:} the residual localizes to the base model's pretrained
political priors, which survive light LoRA fine-tuning, concentrating in
valence-interpretation events. Disclosed as a limitation, not tuned away.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Additive independence / ecological fallacy} (Robinson 1950): the
        three strata are combined additively with no cross-tabulation; $\mu$ and
        $V_{eq}$ coincide only under this approximation.
  \item \textbf{Model-internal win threshold:} $V_{eq}$ is an EC-adjusted
        effective-loyalty threshold, not a literal popular-vote count.
  \item \textbf{Residual base-model lean ($+0.028$):} survives corpus rebalancing;
        disclosed rather than tuned away.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic-label provenance:} labels are LLM-generated
        (DeepSeek+Gemini); external-API use is a documented deviation from the
        reproducibility rule for this stage.
  \item \textbf{Race-only rebalancing:} only 5 race blocs are optimized; religion/
        gender are fixed weights, and the Monte Carlo treats them as neutral
        ($\mu{=}0.50$), understating uncertainty.
  \item \textbf{Small-sample covariance:} 8 first-differences even after
        shrinkage; thin blocs remain data-limited.
\end{itemize}
